Regional Hospital Digital Records

Project overview, charter and scope

Replacing several legacy clinical systems with one integrated electronic health record, site by site, without interrupting patient care.

Overview

Context

The Regional Hospital Network runs several legacy clinical systems, including patient administration, laboratory, radiology and pharmacy. They differ from site to site and do not share one patient record.

Problem

Clinicians switch between systems and chase information across sites. That slows care, raises the risk of identification and medication errors, and makes reporting and audits manual. The legacy systems are also costly and increasingly hard to support.

Solution

Replace them with one integrated electronic health record (EHR) platform. Migrate active patient records, redesign clinical workflows with clinicians, train staff, then go live site by site with a downtime fallback plan.

Purpose

Give clinicians one secure, complete patient record at every site, improving patient safety and continuity of care.

Development approach

Hybrid. Vendor selection, data migration, safety and privacy assurance and site go-lives follow fixed gates, which suits a predictive plan. Workflow configuration benefits from short sprints with clinician feedback, which suits agile.

Charter summary

Major objectives

  • O1 Replace the legacy clinical systems with one integrated EHR at every site.
  • O2 Migrate active patient records accurately and completely.
  • O3 Go live at every site on the baselined schedule and within the approved budget.
  • O4 Meet clinical safety, privacy and regulatory requirements at every gate.
  • O5 Prepare clinical staff before their site goes live.

Scope

In scope

  • Configuring one integrated EHR platform for all RHN sites
  • Migrating active patient records from the legacy systems
  • Interfaces to laboratory, imaging and pharmacy systems
  • Clinical workflow redesign, testing, and safety and privacy assessment
  • Clinician training and super-user support
  • Staged site go-lives, hypercare and closure

Out of scope

  • New clinical services or building works
  • Replacing medical devices, except where needed for integration
  • Digitising historical paper archives
  • Network upgrades, handled by the IT infrastructure programme
  • A patient-facing portal or mobile app, planned as a later phase

Assumptions

  • Funding and executive sponsorship are confirmed before planning starts.
  • Clinical leads can release staff for design, testing and training.
  • Legacy data can be extracted in a usable form.
  • The chosen vendor can supply specialists on time.

Constraints

  • Patient safety comes first, so go-lives avoid peak clinical periods and need a downtime fallback.
  • Patient data must meet applicable health privacy regulations.
  • The sponsor sets a fixed budget envelope.
  • Clinical staff are shared with live patient care.

Critical path

The critical path runs from the charter to project closure through data migration, testing and the site rollout. It has no slack, and 13 of the 20 tasks are on it.

1.1 › 1.2 › 2.1 › 2.2 › 3.1 › 4.2 › 5.1 › 6.1.1 › 6.1.2 › 7.1.2 › 7.1.3 › 7.2.2 › 7.2.3

Key dependency

User acceptance testing (5.1) cannot start until trial migrations reconcile (4.2), because clinicians must test against real migrated records. Trial migrations in turn wait for core configuration (3.1). That chain carries the whole plan into pilot training and the go-live waves.

If a critical activity slips

Every day lost on 4.2 moves all sites live and closure by the same day. A 10-day slip would move all sites live from 19 Nov 2027 to 3 Dec 2027 and closure to 21 Jan 2028. Keeping the go-live and hypercare teams engaged would add roughly $90,600 to $144,400. A slip beyond about 16 working days would breach the 5% schedule tolerance set in Section 1. The pilot go/no-go has only 5 days of slack, so a safety or privacy finding there would also delay the pilot.

Cost and resources

Each task cost is its duration, times the hours per day, times the units of each assigned role, times that role's hourly rate, plus any fixed cost. Longer tasks and larger teams cost more, so a schedule change is also a cost change.

Example, task 5.1: 40 days × 8 hours × $500 combined hourly rate (2 test analysts, 1 business analyst, 3 super-users, half a vendor consultant) = $160,000.

Key cost assumptions

  • Rates are fully loaded hourly rates, with 8-hour days and no public holidays.
  • Clinical super-users are costed as backfill for their normal clinical duties.
  • Licences are first-year costs, and later years fall to operations.
  • Devices are bought per go-live wave, and no inflation is applied.
  • The contingency reserve sits outside task budgets. All figures are group planning estimates, not vendor quotes.
